What is Pauper?

Pauper is a way to play Magic: The Gathering, the world's original trading card game, where every card in your deck has to be a common. Commons are the most-printed cards in every pack. Building with only commons takes the arms race out of deckbuilding, so games come down to skill, smart choices and a little luck.

Skill over spending

Nobody can buy their way past you with a stack of rare cards. Everyone draws from the same pool of commons.

Real Magic

Same rules, same strategy, and many of the game's most famous cards, like Counterspell and Lightning Bolt, were printed as commons.

A welcoming crowd

Pauper players love teaching the format. Most store events have regulars who will walk a newcomer through their first games.

Magic in five minutes

Never played Magic? Here's everything you need to follow a game.

  1. You're a wizard with a deck of spells

    Each player brings their own deck of at least 60 cards and starts at 20 life. Bring your opponent to 0 and you win.

  2. Lands make mana

    Once per turn you can play a land. Tap your lands for mana, the energy you spend to cast spells. Magic has five colors of mana, and each has its own personality:

    • White order, protection, small armies
    • Blue knowledge, trickery, counterspells
    • Black ambition, sacrifice, destroying creatures
    • Red speed, fire, direct damage
    • Green nature, big creatures, extra mana

  3. Cast spells

    Every other card is a spell. The main kinds are:

    • Creatures stay on the table to attack and block.
    • Instants are one-time effects you can cast any time, even on your opponent's turn.
    • Sorceries are one-time effects you cast on your own turn.
    • Enchantments and artifacts stay on the table with a lasting effect.

  4. Take your turn

    Untap your cards, draw a card, play a land and cast spells, then attack with your creatures. Your opponent's creatures can block. Any damage that gets through comes off their life total. Then it's their turn.

That's the core of it. The fun is in the decisions: when to attack, what to hold back, and how to answer your opponent's plan. The best way to learn is to play a few games with someone who knows. That's what local events are for.

How to tell if a card is common

Every Magic card shows its rarity in the color of its set symbol, and newer cards also print it as a letter in the bottom-left corner. In Pauper, you look for a black symbol or a C.

  1. Name. Every card has a unique name.
  2. Mana cost. What you pay to cast it. This one costs one blue mana plus one mana of any color.
  3. Art. Just for looks, but it's half the fun.
  4. Type line. What kind of card it is: creature, instant, land and so on.
  5. Set symbol. Shows which set the card is from. Its color is the rarity. Black means common, so this card would be Pauper legal (as long as it isn't on the banned list).
  6. Rules text. What the card does. Italic text below it is flavor text, just for story.
  7. Power / toughness. Creatures only. Power is the damage it deals in combat, toughness is the damage it can take.
  8. Collector info. The small print in the bottom-left corner. On newer cards it includes the rarity letter: C means common, the same as a black set symbol. It also names the artist. See the close-up below.

The bottom-left corner, up close

042/271
Collector number. This is card 42 of the 271 cards in its set.
C
Rarity letter. C common, U uncommon, R rare, M mythic. This tells you the rarity of this printing, just like the set symbol's color.
DFW
Set code. A short code for the set. Ours is made up; a real one looks like C18 for Commander 2018.
EN
Language. EN is English. Cards are printed in many languages and all of them are legal.
Name
Artist. The paintbrush is followed by the name of the artist who painted the card's art.

Older cards don't have this line. Their artist is credited at the bottom center (often as "Illus."), and the set symbol's color is the only rarity marker.

Set symbol colors

  • Black Common. Pauper legal.
  • Silver Uncommon
  • Gold Rare
  • Orange-red Mythic rare

One catch: check Scryfall

Some cards get reprinted at a different rarity later. If a card was printed as a common even once in a paper set or on Magic Online, it's legal in Pauper. So the copy in your hand might have a silver symbol and a U and still be Pauper legal, because a newer printing made it a common.

The rarity on your card only tells you about that printing. To be sure, look the card up on Scryfall and check the Legalities list on its page. If Pauper says Legal, you can play it. You can also add f:pauper to any Scryfall search to see only Pauper-legal cards.

Where Pauper came from

Pauper started as a fan-made idea and grew into a format played in game stores around the world. Here's how it got here.

  1. Late 2000s

    Players invent it

    Magic Online players started building decks with nothing but commons, and community groups like the Pauper Deck Challenge ran their own events.

  2. Dec 1, 2008

    Official online

    Wizards of the Coast made Pauper an official Magic Online format, with its own events and its own banned list.

  3. 2010s

    Spreads to game stores

    Pauper became one of the most-played formats on Magic Online, and players began bringing their decks to local stores, using the online banned list as the rules.

  4. June 2019

    Official in paper

    Wizards sanctioned Pauper for tabletop play. Any card printed as a common in a paper set or on Magic Online became legal, so paper and online players share one card pool.

  5. January 2022

    The community gets a voice

    Wizards formed the Pauper Format Panel: Gavin Verhey and six well-known Pauper players from around the world who help guide bans and keep the format healthy.

  6. 2026

    DFW Pauper

    Dallas-Fort Worth players start listing every local Pauper event in one place, so it's easy to find a table near you.

New players

Getting started with Pauper

If you can build a 60-card deck, you can play Pauper. The only twist is that every card has to have been printed as a common.

The rules

  • A card is legal if it was printed at common rarity in a paper Magic set or on Magic Online. Scryfall's legal:pauper search shows them all.
  • At least 60 cards in the main deck, up to 15 in the sideboard.
  • No more than 4 copies of a card, except basic lands.
  • The cards on the banned list are not allowed.

Why people love it

  • Competitive decks usually cost a fraction of Modern or Pioneer decks.
  • A diverse meta: aggro, tempo, combo and big ramp are all viable.
  • Classic cards like Counterspell, Lightning Bolt and Snuff Out are legal.
  • It's played on Magic Online every day, so the meta is well documented.

How a Pauper event works

  • Arrive about 15 minutes early and register at the counter. Most events don't need a pre-signup, but check the event's notes.
  • Pay the entry fee, usually $5 to $10.
  • You play 3 or 4 rounds of Swiss, so you face a new opponent each round with a similar record.
  • Each match is best of three games. You can swap sideboard cards in between games.
  • Most stores give a pack or store credit for each match you win, so newer players still walk away with prizes.

What to bring

  • A Pauper-legal deck of at least 60 cards.
  • Up to 15 sideboard cards (optional for your first event).
  • Card sleeves.
  • Dice or counters, and a pen and paper or app for life totals.
  • The entry fee.

Good first decks

Any deck on the meta list can win a local event. These are the easiest places to start.

Fast and simple

Mono Red Rally or Madness

Straightforward aggro: play creatures, attack, finish with burn. Easy to learn and quick to play.

Simple creatures

Elves

Flood the board with small elves, make lots of mana and overwhelm your opponent. Easy to pilot, with plenty of room to improve.

Grow into it

Dimir Faeries or Mono Blue Terror

Tempo decks built on counterspells and flying threats. They take more practice, and they teach you the format faster than anything else.

Your first deck in four steps

  1. Pick a style

    Look at the current meta and choose what sounds fun. Aggro decks like Mono Red Rally or Elves are simple to pilot. Price out a list before you buy (see the next two steps).

  2. Grab a list

    Copy a recent winning list from MTGGoldfish into Moxfield or Archidekt to see the price and swap cards you already own.

  3. Buy the cards

    Support your local game store (LGS) first. They're the ones hosting Pauper events, and buying singles there keeps those events going. Yes TCG is a fantastic resource for finding the cards you need in stock at local stores. If you still need something, TCGplayer and Card Kingdom carry most Pauper staples.

  4. Come play

    Show up to any event on our list. Ask in the local Pauper Discord to borrow a deck first. Regulars are happy to lend one.
